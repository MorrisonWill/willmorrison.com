# Japanese

Notes on learning Japanese. Started September 2024, quit January 2025, and resumed August 2026.

## Returning to Japanese

<p class="entry-meta">53 days: Aug 31-Oct 22, 2026</p>

Coming back to Japanese after close to two years, I contemplated my resource options carefully. I learned from last time that doing too many things leads to burnout. I believe I got so excited with Japanese that I did too much and burned out without laying the groundwork for consistent, burnout-resistent study.

I chose to use the [JLAB](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/911122782) beginner course along with [Tae Kim's Guide](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/) as my primary resources. After 53 days I have done 2,990 reviews in the Anki deck, or around 56 per day. I have 635 cards either mature or learning.

Initially, I decided to go through RTK as well, but I dropped it. I believe I don't have enough issues recogniing kanji to warrant the time it takes to go through. My reviews were quite slow and painful.

I've done some immersion with YouTube videos and podcasts. Some creators I've learned from include OkkeiJapanese, けんさん, いろいろな日本語, and Japanese with Shun.

Progress feels smooth. I'm enjoying the audio cards.

My goal is to get through JLAB's entire beginner course (listening comprehension and reading practice anki decks), and then begin mining while working on his intermediate courses.

<p class="entry-meta">Motivation: high</p>

## Week 1: 09/14/24 - 09/25/24

My first week of Japanese has gone quite well:

- Set up [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/), [Yomitan](https://yomitan.wiki/), and [asbplayer](https://github.com/killergerbah/asbplayer)
  - Learned all Hiragana and 15 Katakana
  - 80 [Kaishi 1.5k](https://github.com/donkuri/Kaishi) cards
  - 100 [RRTK](https://mega.nz/file/2SJiWC4b#hL98qtC_hiLlQDg0LqVJoqD2-5ywT2Nwd4kjROY_KwQ) cards
  - 5 [Cure Dolly](https://www.youtube.com/@organicjapanesewithcuredol49) videos
  - 4 episodes of のんのんびより
  - Some miscellaneous listening trying to find good resources

Anki reviews are taking ~1 hour each day.

Learning Japanese vocabulary is challenging. With Latin and Spanish I could cram tons of words before an exam, but I feel like these Japanese words are taking days to really sink in. My plan is to continue going through Kaishi 1.5k and RRTK at 10 cards/day each. RRTK seems to be paying off quickly: each day kanji look less foreign to me.

I'm glad I chose Cure Dolly as my primary grammar resource to start with. Her explanations seem to suit my brain nicely. My approach has been to watch her videos as well as read the [transcript](https://kellenok.github.io/cure-script/) multiple times. I also found [Jouzo Juls](https://www.youtube.com/@JouzuJuls) on YouTube who seems to publish paraphrased (plagiarized?) versions of Dolly's videos, so I have watched those as well to have a slightly different explanation for each of the concepts.

For immersion, I haven't found a reading resource that is enjoyable and beneficial yet. Watching anime with English subtitles first and then in Japanese has been my primary approach to immersion.

のんのんびより is very fun.

I'm trying to get to the point of being able to consume enjoyable native content as quickly as possible. I know that my motivation will dip in the future, but I think if I can push through to that point then I will continue for a long time.
